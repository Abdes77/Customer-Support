## Second attempt: the race test that passed only most of the time

The orchestrator's `pnpm verify` failed once on "lets only one of two customers take the same
time" (`expected [ '2026-11-12T14:00Z', …(2) ] to not include '2026-11-12T14:00Z'`).

**Confirmed cause.** The test booked 14:00 for two customers with `Promise.all`, then asked
for times *for the second customer* and expected 14:00 to be missing. It assumed the first
customer always wins the lock. Unloaded, the first one did win (15/15 sampled runs); with the
CPU loaded the second won 2 times out of 15, and exactly those 2 runs failed. When the second
customer wins, the offer is for the customer who *has* 14:00: `offer_times` treats that as
moving their meeting, drops their own event from the busy times (as intended, so the times
around it stay open) and then offered 14:00 itself back as a "new" time. So the booking side
was safe (one applied, one rejected, one calendar event in every run; the hold is checked
under the advisory lock in READ COMMITTED, so the second sees the first's committed hold); the
test's assumption was wrong, and the product had a defect: a customer moving a meeting was
offered the time they already have. The slot id dated 2026-11-12 is the fixed test date
(Thursday after `NOW` = 2026-11-10), not the real date.

Reproduced deterministically before fixing: booking the second customer first and then the
first, then offering to the second, failed every time with the same message. The new tests
below fail every time on the old code (all four checked by putting the old code back).

**Real-clock dependence found on the way (product).** A pending hold stopped counting 15
minutes after `bookings.created_at`, which is the database's `now()` (transaction start, cf.
decision 60), compared with the service's clock. The tests use an injected clock
(2026-11-10) behind the real date, so holds always counted; with a real date earlier than the
injected clock (or a clock skew in production) holds would have been treated as stale and two
customers could both be held. Also, an unfinished hold resumed at a new time kept its old
`created_at`, so a hold older than 15 minutes was stale the moment it took its new time and
another customer could take that time while its calendar write ran.

**Fix.**
- `packages/scheduling/src/server/bookings.ts`: a hold carries `metadata.heldAt` from the
  service's clock (read under the lock), written on a new hold and again when an unfinished
  hold takes a new time; `otherBookings` ages pending holds by
  `coalesce(metadata->>'heldAt', created_at)` against the same clock (`clock()`, not the turn's
  `now`). `offer_times` for a customer with a confirmed meeting leaves out that meeting's own
  start. Decision 207; ARCHITECTURE §2 "As built (C12)" updated.
- `scheduling.db.test.ts`: the race test now decides the winner (the loser's calendar read is
  held back by `holdBack()` until the winner has booked, so only the check under the lock
  stops it, with "Someone else just took that time") and runs both orders; it checks the
  loser is not offered the time or its buffer, and the winner is not offered its own time to
  move to. A separate test still lets both book at once and checks whichever lost. New:
  "ages a hold that never finished by the service's clock, not the database's" (14 and 16
  minutes by `heldAt`, whatever the row's real `created_at`) and "holds a resumed attempt's
  new time from when it is resumed" (another customer is refused 11:00 while the resumed
  hold's calendar write is held back).

**Other tests checked for clock and timing.** `availability.test.ts` and `providers.test.ts`
use fixed instants passed in; every database test passes the fixed `NOW` both as the turn's
`now` and as the service clock, OAuth state, token expiry, Calendly signatures and reminder
planning all read that clock, and the jobs' `step.sleep` is a no-op. No source path in the
package reads the database clock any more (the only `Date.now()` is Retry-After's HTTP date,
which is the vendor's real time by nature and not exercised by tests). The only real-clock
test of the chunk is `apps/web/src/server/scheduling/scheduling.test.ts` (the real
composition); it uses times relative to `Date.now()` with minutes of margin, so it does not
depend on the date. The shared `sent`/`enqueued` arrays are only read within one test, and
tests in a file run in order.
